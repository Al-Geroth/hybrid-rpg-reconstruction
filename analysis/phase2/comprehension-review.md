# First-reader comprehension review

An independent AI reviewer received only the introductory handbook, with no Phase I history, catalogue, glossary, rulebook or mathematical register. The reviewer was asked to approach it as an experienced tabletop player new to HYBRID. This was an internal comprehension test. No external human playtest was conducted.

| Question | What the reader could explain before revision |
| --- | --- |
| What kind of game is this? | A game that compares magnitudes across characters and worlds, with its strongest examples in superhero fiction. |
| What is on a sheet? | A baseline with a class and statistic, powers and their particular PL or LS, and a derived average for Mind, Body and Soul. The reader correctly avoided assigning three individual scores or turning DP into hit points. |
| What does changing scale do? | Regular translation retains capability. Reusing the numeral in another class during a transformation can change capability. C0 needs the particular statistic. |
| How do you make a character? | The reader reproduced the 9, 18, 27, 36 mutant progression and identified the benchmark and optional die start. The numbered sequence left some doubt over whether a rolled character should also buy the advantages. |
| What does the player do? | Declare a specific feat. The GM establishes scene facts and identifies a suitable model. The group uses the result to the extent that it answers the question. |
| How does a feat resolve? | A pace requirement can resolve without dice. A percentage for phasing describes an effect. Local roll-high and roll-under conventions can answer particular questions. |
| Where do the original rules stop? | Winning against an opponent, turning a damage total into injury, recovering and earning advancement require further procedures. |

## Transfer test and revision

The reader invented a river pilot who rolled 20 C1 and independently obtained about 49.28 C2 DP and 7.02 average Mind, Body and Soul. On a level towpath the pilot could keep pace with a rescue boat at 10 mph under the ordinary running rule. The reader correctly distinguished keeping pace from catching a boat that started ahead, jumping aboard, or lifting its occupant. The draft gave too little information to repeat the calculation for a new speed.

The handbook now gives the running relation in both directions, an additional 13 mph example, explicit skip instructions for all three starting routes, the class labels in the optional 3d6 examples, a key to A/B/C/D at first use, and a link to the original construction menus. It also explains that a particular source rule must authorize a magnitude-changing relabelling.

## Reread without the catalogue

The reviewer answered a new 25 mph question with a requirement of 23.9794 C2 DP or PL. A runner at 20 C2 reaches 10 mph using ordinary running. The reviewer independently identified the alternate outcome if the character qualifies for the mutant movement procedure. They correctly explained why an 18 C1 mutant and an 18 C2 human can have the same 3d6 distribution without equal magnitude, why a rolled baseline skips the mutant construction, and why a player cannot relabel a stat solely to improve the story. The reader reported no remaining blocker on these tasks.
