# Independent reading of the reconstruction

The reviewer worked on the reading paths and scenario trials. This review concerns the catalogue, glossary, mathematical register and graph prepared by other contributors. Checks on the reviewer's own manuscript are identified below. This is an internal critical review.

## Which evidence was checked

The final review on 23 November 2026 used the 13 February 2003 archive replay of v0.30, 431,957 bytes, SHA-256 `b71d98a0582301d4cb2f83efbb193bfe6c71fa6ccb56e068a4fc14ae553c73ec`. The edition also includes 70 glossary terms, 20 recorded tensions and seven scenario trials. A separate Python standard-library HTML parser decoded the local archival bytes, omitted scripts, inserted boundaries at paragraphs and line breaks, and normalized whitespace. This gave 299 nonempty text blocks. Selecting blocks that begin with a case-insensitive rule heading, with or without the hash sign, gave 204. Their identifiers run from 0 through 203 in document order, with no missing integer and no duplicate. Rule 109 omits the hash sign. The 203 in the masthead matches the last number. It does not match the number of headings when zero is included.

The substantive check used purposive counterexamples. It included local computations, optional character generation, rule revisions, borrowed terms, dangerous and prejudicial extrapolations, pronouncements without procedures, and rules that change subjects midway. It was not a second complete classification of the edition.

## Checks on the interpretation

A second calculation of the Rule 45 inverse verified both branches at representative positive outputs. For an output of 10, inputs 10 and 0.1 both work. Inverting a result above one gives a choice until the domain of the character rating is specified. The registry correctly retains the smaller branch. Rule 188's worked example belongs to the range of inputs greater than one.

The author's 66 percent in Rule 35 and two thirds in the introductory material agree at 3d6 if each is taken as a fraction of the maximum. Exhaustive enumeration gives 81 qualifying totals among 216 equally likely ordered outcomes. This assumption produces a success probability of 37.5 percent. At a maximum of 150, one threshold is 99 and the other is 100. The source leaves the percentage's denominator implicit. A game-wide probability or single target would overstate the evidence. Rule 188 also says that the baseline can differ from ten. Its final worked example uses ten.

Other counterexamples survived independent arithmetic checks. Rule 53's triangular expression gives 120 percent when the total is 150 and the base is 10. Rule 145's own proposed meeting point at technology level 6 produces C2 PL 36 by one expression, while converting 36 back by the other gives 3. Rule 200 can assign a finite fictional rating to a symbol, but conventional powers of two have no finite limit as the exponent grows. None of these examples justifies replacing the printed formula silently.

Glossary checks traced DP to default psyche, MAG to a hypothetical fractional success measure, AMBS to an average, and DCV to several competing HERO comparisons in Rule 189. The mathematical meaning of a square root did not entitle the editors to assign individual Mind, Body and Soul scores. The revised glossary includes COM for a vehicle's size in Rules 155 and 158 and the explicit collision of PC meanings in Rule 50. The revised threshold entry marks the maximum-denominator interpretation as C. Its roll-under counterpart now specifies that the baseline varies.

The graph should distinguish a cited passage, a mathematical prerequisite and an editorial connection. In particular, Rule 35 states a percentage but does not supply the denominator in the normalized threshold. Opposite directions of roll in two rules can be observed without claiming that both govern every combat action. The handbook reports successful local calculations even where a complete contested encounter stays unresolved.

The graph was revised after review. All 528 parsed reference mentions matched numbered targets. The 451 mentions from 140 numbered sources became 383 directed pairs after collapsing 68 repeats. The 77 mentions from 24 distinct unnumbered segments became 65 directed pairs after collapsing 12 repeats. Keeping the unnumbered segments distinct is important because the introductory examples, reviews and preface have different purposes. Removing 23 self-pairs from the 448 distinct pairs leaves 425 pairs for reference centrality. Rule 3 received references from 18 distinct other sources. The typed graph now reports per-relation candidate counts, duplicate pairs, excluded self-links, eligible endpoint types and incoming-degree rankings. The roll-high edge from Rule 35 is identified as interpretive. A distinct edge records actual conflicting point estimates in Rule 122.

## Sensitive material and redistribution

I inspected the public summaries for Rules 4, 7, 34, 36, 80, 88, 98, 110, 118, 180, 191, 193, 196, 198, 199 and 203 against their primary context. The catalogued real-world military extrapolations describe the existence and purpose of claims at a historical level. Numerical character and dice examples in the registry concern fictional capabilities. The account of discriminatory claims marks their lack of empirical support. Private source HTML must remain outside the published tree. An additional overlap check compared every window of 12 successive normalized alphanumeric tokens in the visible text of 23 generated HTML pages against the reference witness. All 23 pages returned zero matches both with every visible field included and after removing 304 machine-generated text-locator fields from the catalogue. HTML attributes, scripts and styles were excluded in both passes. The generated tree contained no source replay or full-text dump. This test cannot decide third-party rights or identify every possible short quotation.

## Recheck of the reviewer's own material

The rulebook gained the optional d20 starting procedure in Rule 107 and the conditional post-death waiting interval in Rule 87. Both qualify broad claims about what the system lacks. The trial of damage stipulates a hit so that its independent failure to determine recovery is observable. The camouflage trial ends when the character acquires the named trait. It does not claim to complete a detection contest. The running test uses an ordinary runner exactly at the required rating.

The substantive verdict is to retain the qualified reconstruction. Marking every equation as an executable game mechanic would overstate playability. Calling every rule unusable would erase specified character choices, a repeatable rating conversion, a local roll target and an ordinary running benchmark. A second reader can repeat these procedures while having no source-defined way to settle the complete duel tested here.
