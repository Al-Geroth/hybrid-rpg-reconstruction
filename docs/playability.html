At the table

Seven playability trials

Each trial starts with a declared task and ends at the first decision the sources cannot settle, or at a reproducible result.

Partially executable

Create an ordinary human courier

A named human with a baseline and a derived average can be made reproducibly. A complete playable courier with a unique allocation and skill set cannot.

Inputs

  • A GM running an ordinary-human campaign on the C2 DP scale (Rule 11).
  • The 16 C2 DP ordinary benchmark in Rule 49. The courier's name and occupation are editorial choices.

Procedure

  1. Use 16 C2 DP as baseline. Calculate AMBS = √16 = 4.
  2. Convert to about 28.18 C3 DP using Rule 45. Retain the C2 DP original on the sheet.
  3. Rule 47 calls for age, COM and the remainder for powers. Attempt to fill these and to identify the character's occupational skills.
  4. Rule 107 can instead generate a baseline by rolling dice for an optional d20 version. It attaches the roll to C1 and describes low-level mutants. It does not complete the missing ordinary courier skill allocation.
Missing
  • A general method connecting this baseline to a free selection of skills and their individual costs.
  • An instruction assigning a mandatory COM expenditure for this build or integrating Rule 79's ideal ratio.
Tensions
  • Rule 182 gives 25 C3 DP as a normal benchmark. Its positive higher-valued C2 inverse is about 15.22. The two normal examples are close but distinct, and neither is identified as universal.
Assumptions
  • The chosen campaign uses Rule 49's benchmark. The GM has authority over the campaign scale.

Executable

Build a low-powered character with camouflage

The stated build goal is reached. The named powers and final baseline are recoverable. The same selections produce the same output.

Inputs

  • Rule 123's low-powered mutant starting package at 9 C2 DP.
  • Select agility, increased height and camouflage in that order from its tier-specific lists.

Procedure

  1. First advantage multiplies 9 by 2, giving 18 C2 DP.
  2. Second advantage multiplies 18 by 3/2, giving 27 C2 DP.
  3. Third advantage multiplies 27 by 4/3, giving 36 C2 DP. Write the three selected traits on the sheet.
  4. Use Rule 49 to obtain AMBS 6.
Missing
  • The build does not specify how an observer detects this camouflage. That would be a separate action test.
Tensions
  • Rule 141 offers another initial-advantage calculation. Rule 123 explicitly supplies its own starting number and multipliers for this template.
Assumptions
  • The character belongs to the type in Rule 123. Choosing among permitted options is a player decision explicitly provided by the passage.

Executable

Keep pace with a float at 10 mph

Yes for the specified pace. No referee-selected numerical parameter or die roll enters the calculation.

Inputs

  • An ordinary runner with 20 C2 DP.
  • A constant pace of 10 mph. The question concerns the pace itself.

Procedure

  1. Rule 39 gives the required C2 rating as 10 × (1 + log₁₀(v)), using mph.
  2. At 10 mph the rating is 10 × (1 + 1) = 20 C2 DP or PL.
  3. The runner has exactly the required rating. The stated pace is within this character's capability in this model.
Missing
  • An endurance limit or a duration before fatigue would be needed for a different question about a long journey.
Tensions
  • Rule 64's separate movement model is expressly for mutants or metahumans. This ordinary runner was selected to avoid that overlap.
Assumptions
  • Constant-speed travel on an unobstructed route is part of the scenario. No general-purpose skill procedure is added.

Ambiguous

Decide a race between a human and a mutant

No unique race outcome. The difference appears before any die roll or fatigue calculation.

Inputs

  • A human and a mutant, each at 20 C2 DP, with equal starts and a straight, unobstructed course.
  • A comparison of running capacity. Both contestants use their full rated pace.

Procedure

  1. Using Rule 39 for both runners, solve 20 = 10 × (1 + log₁₀(v)). Each has v = 10 mph.
  2. For the mutant, Rule 45 also gives about 49.28 C3 DP. Rule 64 directly associates the mutant's C3 number with mph.
  3. The shared running relation yields a tie. Under the mutant movement relation the mutant wins. Rule 35 supplies an individual success criterion without a tie-break for this comparison.
Missing
  • A statement specifying whether Rule 64 supersedes Rule 39 for a mutant running on foot.
  • A simultaneous-finish or opposed-success procedure.
Tensions
  • Two readings assign different speeds to the same contestant, 10 and about 49.28 mph.
Assumptions
  • Rule 64 describes movement broadly. Treating it as an applicable running speed is a hypothetical reading.
  • Instant attainment of rated pace is held equal for comparing the two procedures.

Non-executable

Resolve a fictional duel through its final turn

Individual numbers and even a possible successful test are available. A winner and final state for this duel cannot be derived.

Inputs

  • Two fictional contestants, one at 18 C2 DP for the Rule 17 example, each trying to outlast the other.
  • A declared action and, for illustration, an already-observed 3d6 total of 12.

Procedure

  1. Rule 17 gives the 18-rated contestant three six-sided dice. If the 66 percent in Rule 35 is taken against 18, a 12 reaches the threshold.
  2. Rule 2 offers another success figure from the contestant's C2 DP. The edition gives no handoff establishing that this figure modifies that roll.
  3. Rule 188 supplies local roll-under tests, while Rule 189 offers alternative DCV values. Attempt to choose the acting order, compare a resolved action with a specific defense, record its effect and start the next turn.
Missing
  • An acting-order rule and an identified relation between these success and defense measures for this exchange.
  • A state transition for the opposing character, a next-turn rule and a stopping condition.
Tensions
  • The introductory combat example calls Rules 17 and 188 contradictory and prefers 188. Its roll-under example concerns obtaining a state, so the preference still leaves this duel without a full exchange.
Assumptions
  • The 3d6 total illustrates the threshold reading. No percent die, opposed roll or imported HERO attack matrix is supplied.

Partially executable

Trace a fictional impact through injury and recovery

The pool and its observed total are determinate. The target's changed condition and recovery are unresolved.

Inputs

  • An agreed successful fictional power effect from a mutant with 20 C2 DP.
  • An observed four-die result of 3, 3, 4 and 4. A hit is stipulated to isolate the consequence stage.

Procedure

  1. Rule 146 gives (20/10)² = 4 damage dice. The observed total is 14.
  2. Check Rule 139's treatment of DEF and BOD and Rule 161's comparison to life and hit points in other games for an instruction to update an ordinary target.
  3. Try to determine the target's injury, available next action and recovery time. The cited models do not complete those steps.
Missing
  • A general procedure mapping this damage total and the target's defense to an injury or loss of capacity.
  • An ordinary healing rate or schedule, including when a character can act again.
Tensions
  • Rule 73 describes a one-time maximum calculated from C3 PL. A different rating and purpose prevent silently substituting its pool for Rule 146's.
  • Rule 79 defines health in terms of physical attributes but does not specify a pool from which to subtract 14.
Assumptions
  • The illustrative totals are given inputs. Healing powers named in Rules 123 and 141 are not automatically available to the target.

Non-executable

Carry a character forward and award advancement

Existing numbers can be carried forward. No repeatable award or post-session change follows from the specified adventure.

Inputs

  • The camouflage character ends a session at 36 C2 DP.
  • One completed adventure. To test persistence separately, consider a creator with an active Rule 45 effect.

Procedure

  1. Copy the existing 36 C2 DP rating onto the next session's record. This is ordinary bookkeeping.
  2. Look for a rule translating the completed adventure into a points award, new advantage or higher baseline.
  3. Rule 123's fourth advantage would produce 45 C2 DP during character construction. Rule 125's letters compare skill and experience grades in another game. Neither sets an adventure reward.
  4. In the parallel Rule 45 case, the creator can regain an earlier status when an effect expires. The time of expiry is unspecified.
  5. Rule 87 also gives a conditional post-death wait before receiving a new body. Neither the end of an adventure nor ordinary earned advancement triggers that rule.
Missing
  • A trigger for earning advancement, the size of an award and a method for spending it in later sessions.
  • A common expiry or recovery clock for ongoing effects.
Tensions
  • A creation step, a comparative grade, an effect expiring and the edition's publication calendar describe four different sorts of change. None is identified as a general advancement procedure.
Assumptions
  • Persistence of a written rating is separated from advancement. No points are invented for completing the adventure.
