Critical assessment

Critical assessment

What survives the editorial cut

HYBRID works best when the question already has a shape. Given a runner's pace, it returns a rating. Given a creator's rating and the desired strength of a copy, it can return a count. Given three listed choices, it can produce a mutant with named powers. Those are genuine contributions to play. Faced with two players pursuing incompatible goals, the referee also needs to know which rule has authority and what the world looks like afterward. That layer is much thinner.

A common scale has real explanatory power

Rules 45 and 46 explain a recurring reading error. Doubling 20 C2 before conversion gives roughly 368.63 C3. Converting 20 C2 first and doubling gives roughly 98.56 C3. A difference that appears arbitrary on first reading can therefore have a specific cause. The scale map connects several character and power examples. It cannot derive the modifier for a particular body type, the acceptable target for a skill roll or a victory condition.

The author often starts at the other end

Ordinary game design usually lets the player choose a character and see what happens. Much of HYBRID starts with something that has already happened in a comic or film and seeks a rating that could account for it. Rule 123 works from a cast of existing characters. Rule 166 gives a special adjustment for a robot leader. This is a productive approach to adapting a favourite story. When an unknown character appears in play, the next adjustment is no longer supplied by the known story.

Credit and originality

The author names other games and distinguishes HYBRID's CA DP from Marvel SAGA's own terms in Rule 125. Rule 182 adapts particular GURPS starting totals, while Rule 189 considers several analogues of a HERO statistic. These references establish the author's comparative project. They provide no basis for claiming that the cited games themselves secretly implement this scale. The distinctive contribution observable here is the repeated attempt to translate disparate fictional magnitudes into one personal rating vocabulary.

Source passages · #45 · #125 · #182 · #189

Three different failures of agreement

Some alternatives change the rule's subject, as when a C2 value describes a baseline in one passage and an active power in another. Some give incompatible results even after fixing the subject, as in the overlap between two readings of mutant movement. A third kind is numerical. Rule 188 rounds the converted 35 C3 target to 8 or less, while the Rule 45 conversion yields 7 or less. Each calls for a different editorial treatment.

What removing the digressions costs

Omitting prejudice from worked examples makes the book usable, but would falsify the evidence if it concealed where prejudice shapes inputs. Rules 4 and 199 attach coefficients to demographic categories. These are authorial assumptions and lack a demonstrated empirical basis. Conversely, removing all off-topic material before reading it would lose mechanics. Rule 163 embeds a revision history, and some game reviews and introductory passages explain otherwise obscure scale choices.

Source passages · #4 · #8 · #163 · #182 · #199

The game that remains

The result is a set of superhero character-building templates, scale conversions and local tests suited to a referee exploring what a particular fictional character can do. It supports a small, repeatable exchange about a rated capability. The tested path from opposing intentions through injury, recovery and advancement still needs decisions created at the table. A group could make those decisions and play a campaign. Its house rules would be part of the explanation of how that campaign works.

Contradictions and alternatives

Opposed success directions

author-acknowledged conflict

Rule 17 describes pools, rule 35 requires a high roll, and rule 188 works examples with an inclusive low target. The preface recommends 188 for combat while openly acknowledging the conflict.

The direction and context must be specified before a die total has meaning. The preface gives a preference but no complete unified test.

Source passages · Preface · #17 · #35 · #188

A class conversion changes a roll-under cutoff

arithmetic discrepancy

Rule 188 prints 35 C3 as 17.7 C2. Reversing rule 45 on the increasing branch yields 17.4825728. Subtracting ten and rounding to a whole die value gives seven, while the printed example gives eight.

A roll of 8 succeeds under the worked example and fails under the recalculated threshold.

Source passages · #45 · #188

Two success fractions

wording discrepancy

The preface says two thirds and rule 35 says 66 percent. With an assumed percentage-of-maximum reading, both give a threshold of 12 on 3d6. Their thresholds differ on 25d6, at 100 and 99 respectively.

The larger pool is an editorial stress test, not a source example. The denominator remains unspecified.

Source passages · Preface · #35

Opposite operations preserve different things

underdetermined operation

Under the additive and multiplicative trades, shortening a 40 C2 LS to 20 from an initially equal PL produces 60 or 80 PL. One route preserves a sum and the other a product.

The choices are allowed alternatives. There is no unique PL given only the initial and final LS.

Source passages · #3 · #183

A higher lifespan rating can produce fewer years

branch ambiguity

Reading the 22 − C2 relation as applying below 20 and the multiplier two as applying from 20 gives 129.52 years at 19 C2 LS but 98.56 at 20. The 22 − C2 statement is printed without a restriction.

The piecewise reading reconciles the wording locally and still loses monotonicity. A different calibration would be an editorial amendment.

Source passages · #6 · #31

Several mappings called C0 Psyche

overloaded quantity

C1 DP of 100 can yield 50 C0 by the logarithmic quotient or 100 by identity, depending on the comparison. Other examples use C2/2 or C3/3.

The statistic and type of subject must accompany C0. A universal conversion table would erase source conditions.

Source passages · Preface · #5

C0 immortality has two formulas

author-acknowledged conflict

At 20 C1 DP, equivalent to 49.28095 C2, the two #31 proposals yield about 246.4 and 40. The author discusses the character types for which each might be appropriate.

A proposed domain qualification exists. Rules for selecting the form on every new character do not follow.

Source passages · #6 · #31

Two budgets for one HERO comparison

alternative calibration

At 100 C1 DP the proposed HERO total is 1000 under the linear C1 relation and 1600 under the C3 logarithmic relation. Rule 122 calls the 600-point difference an uncertainty.

A range of estimates cannot determine which budget to spend.

Source passages · #8 · #122

Rounding a baseline changes the next conversion

precision-dependent example

Rule 8 gives 12.54321 C1, 16 C2 and 28.55 C3 together. Full precision gives 16.08799 C2 and 28.55264 C3. Treating the printed integer 16 as exact yields 28.17766 C3.

Carry the unrounded C1 result for cross-scale work. The rounded 16 can still explain the 4 AMBS example.

Source passages · #8 · #45 · #49

Two motion laws in an overlapping context

conditional conflict

If a mutant’s #64 movement includes running, 20 C2 DP gives 10 mph by the inverse of #39 and 49.28 mph by #64 at the equivalent C3 DP.

Mode-specific applicability could reconcile them. The reader cannot infer that restriction from these statements alone.

Source passages · #39 · #64

Two speeds for one warp number

explicit alternatives

Rule 30 permits both 2^z − 1 and 2^(z−1). At z = 2 they give 3 and 2 times light speed.

A warp number and craft size do not select one speed.

Source passages · #10 · #30

Technology formulas fail even at their stated special case

failed equivalence

Rule 145 gives C2 PL = TL² and TL = √(C2 PL)/2. It proposes an exception at TL 6 and C2 PL 36, although the latter formula returns TL 3 there.

The ordinary inverse of the first relation would omit division by two. The source gives no replacement for either equation.

Source passages · #8 · #145

The subtracted damage die

alternative calibrations

For the same 49 C3 PL input, #118’s two named pool readings give 7d6 or 6d6. Rule 146 supplies an additional square-of-C2-DP pool for mutants.

Effect type and a branch must be selected. A claim that every hit uses one universal pool would conceal the conflict.

Source passages · #14 · #73 · #118 · #146

Infinite exponent assigned a finite value

mathematical incompatibility

For an alphabet position of one, #200 assigns a finite 100 CA to a base of two raised to infinity. Ordinary positive integer powers of two grow without bound. The separate class-conversion sequence beginning at 100 CA is algebraically valid.

The first mark can serve as fictional notation. It fails if read as a mathematical limit.

Source passages · #45 · #200

One celestial diameter, multiple numerical inputs

dimensional inconsistency

Rule 25 permits miles or kilometers for diameter in the same logarithmic expression with unchanged coefficient. The numeric rating changes when the same physical length is expressed in the other unit.

A unit must be fixed for reproducible comparisons. No unit-invariant physical law is supplied.

Source passages · #25

Overlapping alphabetic multiplier conditions

branch ambiguity

In #55, index + 1 can be both even and divisible by three. #58 separately fixes ten times the index in one use. The illustrated X, Y and Z do not resolve every possible overlap.

Applying a single alphabet chart to all figures would require undocumented precedence and scope.

Source passages · #55 · #58

Two estimates for the same appearance rating

author-acknowledged revision

At 20 C2 the later rule gives 9 while the earlier C3/(π+1) expression gives about 11.90. Rule 197 expressly compares the two.

The catalogue preserves both as game calibrations. Neither provides evidence about actual people.

Source passages · #124 · #126 · #197

An effect called permanent may wear off

terminological tension

Rule 45 contrasts temporary effects with effects paid for by demoting a creator’s class status. The creator may recover the previous status after a supposedly permanent effect wears off naturally.

An example of changing status exists. Its duration and expiry procedure remain open.

Source passages · #45

A phasing percentage can exceed one hundred

domain conflict

Rule 53 calls the triangular expression a percentage. At total 150 C2 and base 10 it evaluates to 120 percent.

The literal formula works as a scale but needs a bound or a further interpretation before it can represent an ordinary probability.

Source passages · #53 · #68

Relative magnitudes disagree even for equal souls

counterexample to equivalence

At equal C2 DP and the example modifier 1, #34’s comparison yields 0.1. Equal class ratings in a direct ratio give 1. The author recognizes discrepancies between comparison models.

The number of comparable entities and a scale ratio cannot be assumed to mean the same thing.
