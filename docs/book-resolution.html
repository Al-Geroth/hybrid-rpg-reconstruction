Reconstructed rulebook · Chapter 5

Actions, thresholds and contests

A question the rules can answer

A runner needs to match a float travelling at 10 mph. Read Rule 39 in its stated direction. Take the base-ten logarithm of the speed, add one and multiply by ten. The required rating is 20 C2, in DP or PL. A runner already rated at 20 C2 meets this particular requirement. A finish time, fatigue and interference from someone else would each require further information.

B · Reconstructed reading

Source passages · #39

A die roll with a target

Rule 17 optionally derives a die count from a rating. An example at 18 gives three six-sided dice. Rule 35 calls for 66 percent or more on a combat or skill attempt. The introductory disclaimer also describes two thirds. Reading this as a fraction of the maximum total gives 12 or more on 3d6. With fair dice this occurs in 81 of 216 ordered outcomes, or 37.5 percent. The example demonstrates why a success threshold should never be reported as the probability of success. Using the maximum as denominator is an inference.

A second way to roll

Rule 188 distinguishes a creation-only comparison from a target that can be rolled during play. In the latter example the author assigns a target of 11 or less to a character trying to attain a particular state. Later in the same rule a converted target is rounded. This is evidence for an inclusive roll-under test in its stated setting. Extending it to every skill or choosing the dice for every such test requires another decision.

Source passages · #17 · #35 · #188

What happens when two characters compete?

Rule 34 gives a ratio for two rated souls in a particular fictional model. Rule 35 tests success for an individual. Neither passage explains how to resolve two simultaneous successes in an ordinary contest. Even if both participants have a speed, Rule 64 assigns a mutant a speed directly from C3 DP, while Rule 39 prices running from C2. The choice of speed rule can change the outcome of a race.
