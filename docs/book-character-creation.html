Reconstructed rulebook · Chapter 3

Creating a character

Choose the campaign's scale

Rule 11 gives the GM a concrete responsibility. Players begin with DP, and the campaign and GM determine its scale. Rule 182 relates an ordinary character to 25 C3 DP and a heroic one to 100 C3 DP in a comparison with GURPS. Rule 49 offers a different ordinary benchmark of 16 C2 DP. That converts to roughly 28.18 C3 DP. Both work as starting examples. Treating them as the same mandatory starting total would introduce a discrepancy of our own.

Roll a starting magnitude in the optional d20 version

Rule 107 proposes another entry point. Roll one twenty-sided die and read its face as C1 DP. The source also allows pools. In its 3d6 example the faces 6, 6 and 6 give 18 C1 DP, about 37.65 C2 DP. The proposed d20 version therefore contains an explicit random generation procedure even though much of the game is diceless. It supplies a baseline. The rule does not make every character a product of that procedure.

Give the numbers a place on the sheet

Rule 47 asks for the humanoid's name and age, then a COM allocation and the points remaining for powers at a named scale. In Rule 6, a character whose COM consumes the whole example power rating has nothing left for mutant powers. Rule 79 separately gives an ideal ratio of 4 to 8 to 12 for mental attributes, powers and physical attributes. It does not specify a sequence that allocates the Rule 47 budget into all three.

Build one kind of superhuman

Rule 123 supplies an actual sequence of choices. Begin at 9 C2 DP. Choose agility as the first advantage and double the rating to 18. Choose increased height as the second and multiply by one and a half to reach 27. Choose camouflage as the third and multiply by four thirds to finish at 36. These three choices are an editorial selection from the author's lists. Each is available at the selected step. Rule 49 then yields an average Mind, Body and Soul rating of 6.

Where the template stops

Rule 123 gives sufficient steps for that particular build. Rule 141 uses a different way to size an initial advantage from COM and demonstrates increasing a named character's ratings. Rule 47 describes a write-up in terms of points left after an allocation. Applying all three as compulsory steps of a single build could charge for the same ability twice. The edition does not establish that sequence.
