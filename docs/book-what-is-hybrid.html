Reconstructed rulebook · Chapter 1

What is HYBRID?

Start with a character you recognize

Imagine a player asking how a human courier and a comic-book speedster could appear on the same character sheet. HYBRID's answer begins with a rating whose scale travels with the number. The author moves between people, cartoon monsters, vehicles and planets in the same vocabulary. Familiar fictional characters supply benchmarks. A reader is often given the character first and invited to work backward to a rating.

What the author set out to do

The introduction calls Psyche the common statistic, with life-span and power level as two expressions. It recommends a calculator with a base-ten logarithm. In the preface, the author describes trying to reconcile unequal abilities that other games express with similar-looking numbers. Rule 163 tells how the author's IQ formula changed across years. Changes to the formula are evidence of design history. They provide no measurements of intelligence.

How much of a game survives?

Several pages tell a player exactly which calculation to make. A few also identify a successful roll. These procedures have different scopes. Rule 39 can price a running speed in C2 points, while Rule 188 can test a particular state of a character. Neither specifies how a referee should resolve an arbitrary scene. The title page's percentage of completion follows the calendar convention in Rule 0. It cannot tell us what fraction of playable procedures is present.
