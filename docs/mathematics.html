Mathematics

What survives a change of scale? Start with one conversion, then follow the register into lifespan, movement, points and dice.

One number, two scales

Write 20 C2 on a character sheet. Rule 45 lets you express that rating as about 49.28 C3. You gained digits on the page. The character gained no power. Rule 45 also describes a different operation, in which the numeral stays put while the character changes class. Recording the operation alongside the number matters.

Why the curve turns

Write the input as 10 raised to u. The output is then 10 raised to u squared. Inputs 0.1 and 10 both give 10, since −1 and +1 have the same square. The real-valued formula takes positive inputs and yields values of at least one. An inverse therefore needs a branch. The increasing branch matches the ordinary positive ratings above one used in the cited examples.

Testing the height-to-mass table

M = h³

Cubing the given height reproduces the later rows of the source table to its printed precision, but misses the first by a factor of approximately eight.

Numerical check
One meter computes to 35.29 pounds, two to 282.30 and four to 2,258.40. This checks the table’s arithmetic, not a claim about human physiology.
Uncertainty
At one meter the text prints 282.30 pounds. The stated conversion and formula give 35.287552 pounds. The printed value would fit two meters. Relabeling the row is conjectural.
Variables, domain and dependencies
Variables
h is numerical height in feet. M is the proposed mass in pounds. The source uses 3.28 feet per meter and 2.2 pounds per kilogram.
Domain
Positive height in the units chosen by the author.
Dimensions
To map a length cubed to mass requires a density and shape factor. Entering feet and reporting pounds implicitly fixes a coefficient of 1 lb/ft³ in this convention.

A factorial proposed for strength

W = (d/log₁₀ d)!

Rule 78 combines a logarithmic quotient with a factorial, then illustrates four factorial. At a given d these are two distinct operations.

Numerical check
The equality 4! = 24 is correct. Substituting 4 C1 into the printed composite formula does not reproduce that example.
Uncertainty
With d = 4 the inner quotient is about 6.644, not four and not an integer. A gamma extension or a rounding rule would be an editorial addition.
Variables, domain and dependencies
Variables
d is C1 DP. W is a number described as pounds.
Domain

Dimensions
Using factorial on a rating gives a number. Pounds enter by the fictional calibration.
