Reconstructed rulebook · Chapter 6

Combat, damage and recovery

Find the separate quantities

Rule 2 produces a combat success figure called Martial Arts Grade. Rule 17 proposes a die pool, Rule 35 a roll-high criterion, and Rule 188 a roll-under criterion for particular states. The introductory Example 4 prefers Rule 188 for combat and acknowledges disagreement with Rule 17. The preference tells us where the author wanted readers to look. It still leaves the connection between an attacker's test and an opponent's defense unspecified.

Rate a fictional effect

Rule 146 maps a mutant's C2 DP into a count of six-sided damage dice. Its own 20 C2 DP example yields four dice. Rule 73 gives another expression, uses C3 power level and labels the result a one-time maximum. Rule 139 uses a brick character as the basis for relating damage, DEF and BOD. These three passages describe different inputs and circumstances. A difference between two outputs is not by itself an error.

Follow the target beyond the roll

Suppose the four damage dice show a total of 14. The example establishes a numerical result. Rule 189 offers several definitions of a defensive value borrowed from HERO. Rule 161 compares other games' life points with HYBRID's scales. Neither gives a general instruction to subtract those 14 points from a character's DP. Healing is named as a power in Rules 123 and 141. We found no general recovery interval or injury track to carry that target into the following turn.
