Reconstructed rulebook · Chapter 10

Running a scene at the table

Begin with a bounded question

Give the GM and players the starting rating and its scale, the rule to use and the outcome being asked for. The GM's selection of a campaign scale is explicit in Rule 11. For an ordinary running benchmark the remaining inputs can be as small as the speed in mph and the runner's C2 DP. The printed calculation returns a requirement the player can compare with the character's rating.

B · Reconstructed reading

Source passages · #11 · #39

Stop where the fiction needs another decision

A group can perform a short exchange. The player asks whether the runner can keep pace at 10 mph, the GM chooses Rule 39, and the two compare the 20 C2 requirement with the sheet. Introducing a mutant opponent brings Rule 64 into play. Adding a chase through a crowd introduces interruptions and opposition. A GM can adjudicate, but a ruling about those events cannot be recovered from the speed calculation.

Record an editorial choice when one enters play

For a table experiment, record the declared action, chosen rule, input values, intermediate results, and any ruling that changed the fiction. A ruling might settle which movement model applies in a race. This recording practice belongs to the reconstruction. It lets another group repeat a trial and identify the first point where independent procedures diverged.
